# Deload/ Unload options
Whenever deloading is prescribed, whether the 4th week of a standard 3:1 format, or any other context… They will have two options that they can choose from. Will be set at plan generation. 
# Deload Option 1 — Maintain intensity, reduce volume 30–50%
Use when
- Load is moving normally.
- Technique is stable under heavier weights.
- Fatigue is mostly accumulated/global, not joint-specific.
- Skill/sparring load is high and you want to keep strength/power “online.”
## % logic
- Keep roughly the same intensity zone as the block.
- Reduce total sets, total reps, or both.
- Example:
Normal week: 4x4 @ 80%
Deload: 2x4 @ 77.5–80%
- Good fit when % prescriptions are reliable because 1RM is current and daily readiness is not far off baseline.
## RPE logic
- Keep effort moderately high, but below normal peak effort.
- Normal week around RPE 8–9 becomes deload work around RPE 7–8.
- Cut sets first before cutting load.
Why
Preserves neural familiarity with heavier loads.
Maintains force production better than dropping load too much.
Removes fatigue through lower workload, not lower specificity.

# Deload Option 2 — Maintain volume, reduce intensity
Use when
- Heavy loads feel unusually hard.
- Bar speed is down.
- Technique breaks earlier than usual.
- Joints/tendons feel beat up.
- You want to keep training rhythm and technical exposure, but reduce loading stress.
## % logic
- Keep sets/reps similar, reduce load enough to clearly drop stress.
- Usually reduce load by 5–10% for strength/power work while keeping similar sets/reps. Push toward 10–15% only if loads are moving poorly or athlete is clearly worked up/exhausted,, or joints/tendons are beat up, or any other common sensical reason. Larger reductions are generally unnecessary for a standard deload and are more appropriate in more aggressive taper contexts.
- For instance, in a 3:1 format, if you’ve gone for 3x5 at 72.5 > 75 > 77.5 in the first 3 overloading weeks respectively… in the 4th week, that would normally be 70%. 
Option 2 is better than fixed heavy percentages when the athlete is underperforming relative to estimated 1RM.
- The % decline is very contextual. Use common sense based on the input of the athlete. 
## RPE logic
- Keep the same basic structure, but drop effort clearly.
- Normal week around RPE 7–9 becomes deload work around RPE 6–7.
- Example:
Normal week: 4x4 @8-9
Deload: 4x4 @6-7
Why
May reduce joint, tendon, and neural stress more than Option 1.
Keeps movement practice and session flow consistent.
Useful when % work would overshoot because the athlete is not expressing normal strength that week.
