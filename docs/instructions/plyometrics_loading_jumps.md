# Plyometrics and Loaded Jumps
##  Plyometric Exercise Selection & Impact Categorization
Depending on the athlete’s experience and competency and where they are in the program, begin with low impact plyometrics, and progress into medium and high impact. 

Core rule: Start with low-impact plyometrics and only progress to medium or high impact when the athlete has enough training experience, movement competency, and is far enough along in the program phase. ALTERNATIVELY, HAS EXPLICITLY STATED THEY ARE EXPERIENCED IN PLYOMETRICS.

### App rules
#### Rule 1: Default entry point
 Every athlete starts in low impact (unless they clearly and explicitly qualify for a higher tier).
#### Rule 2: Experience gate
 Use training age / plyometric background:
Beginner / little plyometric exposure → low impact


Intermediate / some exposure → low to medium


Advanced / extensive exposure → medium to high, if other criteria are met


#### Rule 3: Competency gate
 Progression is not based on experience alone. The athlete must show:
good landing mechanics


control of knee/hip/trunk positions


ability to absorb force quietly and efficiently


stable single-leg balance where relevant
If competency is poor, keep them at low impact even if they are experienced.
#### Rule 4: Program-phase gate
 Tie impact level to where they are in the cycle:
Early phase → low impact


Middle phase → medium impact


Late / peaking phase → high impact, only if ready


#### Rule 5: “Pass before progress”
 The athlete only moves up if they meet all three:
sufficient experience


sufficient competency


correct phase of program


If one is missing, stay at current tier.
#### Rule 6: No big jumps
 Progress only one level at a time:
low → medium


medium → high
 Never jump beginner athletes straight into high-impact drills.


#### Rule 7: Regression rule
 Move the athlete back down a tier if:
landing quality drops


pain appears


excessive soreness/fatigue occurs


contact times become too slow / movement loses sharpness


#### Rule 8: Exposure before intensity
 Before increasing impact, first ensure the athlete tolerates:
current drill quality


current volume


current frequency


So the app should not only ask, “Can they do it?” but also, “Can they recover from it?”

You now follow a phased plyometric and power-specialization principle: Begin with general plyometrics and medicine ball throws (e.g., depth jumps, broad jumps, chest passes), then transition into more complex and sport-specific variations as the program progresses. For example:
- Boxing: skater jumps, med-ball punches, bag slams.
- Muay Thai/Kickboxing: scissor jumps, single-leg bounds, explosive kicks.
- Wrestling: seated box jumps, resisted broad jumps, med-ball suplex throws.
These evolutions should occur logically across Accumulation → Intensification → Power/Speed → Taper/Peaking phases.

## Back Squat Jumps and Trap-Bar Jumps or Loaded Jumps
For back squat jumps and trap bar jumps, and similar loaded jumps, primary method = % of body mass (BM) for the external load (bar + plates).
Use these ballpark zones, then fine-tune with a quick test (below):
~30–60% BM for most fighters
Note: many hex bars weigh 20–25 kg, and barbells normally 20kg. If the bar alone overshoots your target % for smaller athletes, use a lighter bar or dumbbells. Make sure to emphasize this in the program when these lifts are prescribed.
Going heavier usually just kills flight time and mechanics.
If you’ve got tech: auto-regulate with jump height (contact mat/app) or bar velocity (LPT). Otherwise, simply go by intuition and go full-effort on each jump.
 
## Rep Scheme for Plyometrics and Loaded Jumps
Depending on the nature of the jumping exercise stick to anywhere from 3 reps to 10 reps even. For example, depth jumps are very intense and should normally be done along the lines of 3-5. Pogo jumps are relatively light, and can be done up to 10, and sometimes up to 20. Loaded jumps can be done 3-5 usually. Very rarely (if ever) stay below 3 reps for any jump, as that is too little stimulus and too time consuming with regards to rest periods. Apply similar pragmatic and common sensical thinking.
Simple prescriptions (per set)
Extensive/stiffness work (pogos, ankle jumps): 10–20 reps
Low-hurdle hops or Low Box Jumps: 5-10 reps
Jumps for height/distance (CMJ, box jump, broad jump): 3–6 reps
Depth/shock jumps: 3–5 reps
Loaded jumps (trap-bar/back-squat jumps): 3–5 reps
Progression knobs: Volume (contacts/sets) → Intensity (height/load/approach speed) → Complexity (depth → rebound → hurdle series).
